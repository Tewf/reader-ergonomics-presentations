# 0001: A public standard, and private styles

**Decision:** A person's own way of presenting stays in a style of their own that overrides this repository, which holds the common ground of any presentation.

**Status:** accepted, 2026-10-29; extended by [0006](0006-the-visual-standard-of-the-family.md). **Shape:** [decision record](https://github.com/Tewf/reader-ergonomics/blob/v1.18.2/shapes/decision-record.md).

## Context

The repository began as the talk half of an academic one and widened step by step to any presentation. The maintainer asked what it is for.

## Decision

It holds the common ground of any presentation: principles, contexts, shapes and checks. A person's own way of presenting is a style of their own, kept by them, that overrides this standard, the way a reader style overrides reader-ergonomics.

## Alternatives rejected

- A repository for one person's talks: the evidence is general, and a personal deck would hide which rules are evidence and which are taste.
- One repository per context: academic, business and casual talks share most rules, and three copies would drift.

## Consequences

A principle enters only with evidence from the review. A personal preference, such as a deck framework or a palette, stays out. A test talk by one person tests the standard; it does not set it.

The cost: a personal style carries no evidence of its own, and it can drift from the standard without anyone seeing it.
