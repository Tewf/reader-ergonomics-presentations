# Shape: talk

The shape of a spoken talk with slides: the point first, the plan, one section per claim, the point again, then the questions. The outline is format-neutral. It fits any slide tool, or none.

## When to use it

- A conference or seminar talk, the defence of a thesis or an internship report, a lecture, or a pitch of about ten to forty minutes.
- Not for a deck read without a speaker: the [business decision maker](../reader-styles/business-decision-maker.md) style says how that changes.
- Inside a fixed slot, cut material to fit it. Do not speed up ([academic audience](../reader-styles/academic-audience.md)).
- Length is a convention: Beamer 2026 advises not more than one slide per minute, and Kawasaki 2005 advises 10 slides in 20 minutes (both conventions, untested).

## How each part maps to the evidence

| Part | What it does | Evidence | Grade |
|---|---|---|---|
| Title slide stating the point | gives the one thing to remember in the first minutes | Peyton Jones 1993, TED 2026 (example only) | convention, untested |
| The plan | tells the listener the parts, as an organiser | [overview first](https://github.com/Tewf/reader-ergonomics/blob/v1.0.0/principles/document/overview-first.md); Rowley-Jolivet 2005 (talks open differently from papers) | measured for organisers; supported with caveat for talks |
| One section per claim: sentence headline | states the claim of each slide | Garner 2013 | supported with caveat |
| One section per claim: evidence | shows a figure or table placed with the words that explain it | Garner 2013; [draw relations](https://github.com/Tewf/reader-ergonomics/blob/v1.0.0/principles/page/draw-relations.md) | supported with caveat |
| What to say on each slide | adds to the slide and does not repeat it | Adesope 2012, Yue 2013 | measured |
| Points shown as spoken | builds a figure in step with the voice | Liu 2022, Ito 2026 | supported with caveat (recorded narration only) |
| Gesture | points at what is said | Hostetter 2011, Dargue 2019 | measured |
| Segments of long material | splits the hour into parts | Rey 2019 | supported with caveat (lab lessons) |
| The point again | returns to the point and ends on it | Peyton Jones 1993 | convention, untested |
| Questions and backup slides | prepares the likely questions with slides for them | Xu 2022 (secondary) | convention (the research is descriptive) |

Each key (Author Year) is listed in [bibliography](../evidence/bibliography.md).
No part of this shape has been tested against another talk structure on listener outcomes; [position](../evidence/presentations/position.md) says what is not known.

## Skeleton

Copy this into your slide tool as one outline. The lines after "Say:" are for the speaker, not for the slide.

````markdown
# Slide 1. Title that states the point

On the slide: the title as one sentence stating the point, your name, the venue and the date.

Say: the point in one sentence, and why the listener should care.

# Slide 2. The plan

On the slide: three to five parts, in the order they come, as a short list or a simple map.

Say: what each part does, in one sentence each.

# Slides 3 to n. One section per claim

## Claim slide

On the slide: a headline that is a sentence of at most two lines stating the claim. Below it the evidence, a figure or a table, with the few key terms that explain it.

Say: what the figure shows, where to look, and what follows. Point at the part as you name it. Do not read the slide.

## Evidence build (optional)

On the slide: the same figure, with each part appearing as you reach it.

Say: the step you are on, and pause before the next one.

## Section close

On the slide: the claim again, in a few words.

Say: how this claim supports the point, and where the evidence is weak.

# Slide n+1. The point again

On the slide: the point of slide 1, in the same words.

Say: the point, the parts that supported it, and what follows. End here.

# Slide n+2. Questions

On the slide: the point and a way to reach you.

Say: invite questions. Repeat each question before you answer it.

# Backup slides

On the slide: one slide per likely question, with its headline and its evidence.

Say: nothing until a question needs the slide. Prepare the list of likely questions before the talk.
````
