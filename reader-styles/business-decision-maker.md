# Reader style: business decision maker

How the presentation principles change for a reader who will decide. It has three parts: who the reader is, the overrides, and what is open.

## Who

- **Reader:** someone who will decide on what the presentation asks: an investor, a buyer or a manager. They open the deck, or sit in the room, to decide.
- **Expertise:** expert in their own criteria, often a novice in the speaker's topic.
- **Medium:** a room or a screen. The deck may be read without a speaker.
- **Reading mode:** scanning for the answer and the ask; a deck is read in about three minutes (DocSend 2022).

## Overrides

The base is the academic talk. The business context changes few rules, and each row keeps its grade. Weakest rows: the memo and the story-with-number. Strongest row: the team and preparedness content (Bernstein 2017), though it concerns the first screen of an email.

Supported with caveat (angels); measured (Brooks 2014, US pitch competitions) |

## Thresholds

None changed.

## Open questions

- No experiment compares answer first with background first for a business talk or deck.
- No experiment compares a memo with a deck for comprehension or decision quality.
- Executive and board briefings, and sales presentations: no study found.
- Colour, motion and font size for business: no difference found against the base.
- Kept unchanged here: the sentence headline with a figure as evidence, one claim per slide, legible type and colour not the only cue, rehearsal, a handout of its own, gesture, and segmenting.
