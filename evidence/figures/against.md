# Evidence against and open controversies

This file gathers the evidence against the figure rules: null results, disputed studies, and advice that a study contradicts, by theme.

## Minimalism against embellishment

- Rule under test: remove ink that is not data (Tufte, as reported by Few 2011 and Europa 2023); drop decoration (Rougier 2014).
- Bateman 2010: 20 students; embellished charts were described as accurately and, with 10 participants in the long-term condition, recalled better after two to three weeks. Grade: measured, for the charts of one designer.
  - The embellishments came from one illustrator, and exact values were not read (Kosara 2010); Few 2011 adds that the messages fit in one sentence and the embellishment was relevant to them.
  - Few 2011 restates the result with those limits and criticises the study. Kosara 2010 agrees minimal charts still suit analysis.
- Borgo 2012 (abstract): recall up, visual search slower.
- Skau 2015 (abstract and Kosara's summary): most bar embellishments lowered accuracy when comparing bars.
- Cheng 2026 (held): interesting irrelevant material lowers learning, g = -0.16.
- Reading: decoration that carries the message may help memory; decoration that distorts a quantity or distracts hurts. The rule "remove all decoration" is too broad and "add some" is unsupported for diagrams.

## Preference against performance

- Inbar 2007 (abstract): 87 students preferred a standard bar graph to a minimalist one. Hill 2018 (secondary, from a search summary): charts with lower data-ink ratios were rated better.
- Both measure ratings. Skau 2015 measures accuracy and finds some embellishments cost it.
- Reading: do not take a rating as a reading result.

## Crossings

- Burch 2021 reports a 26-participant study where layouts with crossings were fastest to interpret.
- Purchase 1997 (understanding, abstract) and Ware 2002 (path finding, held, secondary) find the reverse.
- Reading: the task decides. For path finding, avoid crossings or make them wide; for interpretation, crossings may not matter.

## Appeal

- Thielsch 2019 reports g = 0.12 over 25 studies; Schlamann 2026 reports g = 0.29 over 31, and kept only studies whose aesthetic manipulation was confirmed by ratings. Both are heterogeneous, and neither is about figures.
- (not verified: that later work contests the link from beauty to usability; it was named in a summary and not retrieved.)
- Harrison 2015 shows appeal forms in 500 ms and rests largely on colourfulness and visual complexity. That predicts liking, not understanding.
- Song 2025 contradicts itself on image-style bars.

## Flow direction

- Theory favours left to right; Figl 2015 found readers adapt to other directions with 44 participants. A convention, not a result.

## Language models and diagram source

- Das 2024: image form beats text under token limits. Zhong 2024: text beats image on most structure questions, except cycle checks.
- Zou 2024 compares different models. No same-model comparison of an authored diagram as source and as image was found; Zhong 2024 and Das 2024 make it for generated graphs.
- Reading: advice that "models read source better" is untested for authored diagrams.

## Failed or unavailable checks

- Hill 2018 and Huestegge 2011 could not be opened; their numbers are secondary.
- No replication of Bateman 2010 for node-link diagrams was found.
