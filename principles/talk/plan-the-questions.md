# Plan the questions

**Claim:** the questions are part of the talk: the likely ones are prepared, with backup slides.

- **Grade:** convention (the research is descriptive).
- **Evidence:** [review, strand 5](../../evidence/presentations/review.md); [position, Not known](../../evidence/presentations/position.md).
- **Check:** `talk-structure` (heuristic): a questions slide and backup slides after it.
- **Reader styles:** changed by [academic audience](../../reader-styles/academic-audience.md) (peers or a jury) [casual audience](../../reader-styles/casual-audience.md) (questions during the talk, in a small room), and [business decision maker](../../reader-styles/business-decision-maker.md) (the Q&A is part of the decision).

## What it asks

- Leave time for questions, and give the Q&A its own plan (baseline checklist).
- List the likely questions before the talk, and prepare an answer for each.
- Put backup slides after the final slide for the details a question may need.
- Say what the evidence is and where it is weak, so the question is not a surprise.

## Why

Q&A research is sparse and mostly descriptive. In 80 computer science sessions (268 turns), question forms were linked to functions and rapport work (Xu 2022, secondary).

## Limits

- What a Q&A should do for the listener's memory is not known.
- Backup slides have no study found. They are a convention.
- The one measured result concerns investors, not talks in general: Kanze 2018 is described in the business context of the [business decision maker](../../reader-styles/business-decision-maker.md).
