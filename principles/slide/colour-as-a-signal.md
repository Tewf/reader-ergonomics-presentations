# Colour as a signal

**Claim:** colour links what belongs together and is never the only cue; data colour maps are perceptually ordered.

- **Grade:** measured for colour coding and for colour maps.
- **Evidence:** [review, strand 8](../../evidence/presentations/review.md); [position, Known](../../evidence/presentations/position.md).
- **Check:** `deck-judgment` (judgment): does colour link parts, does the slide read in greyscale, is any map rainbow-like?
- **Reader styles:** none changes it.

## What it asks

- Give one colour to one kind of thing, and keep it across the deck.
- Never let colour be the only cue. The slide stays readable in greyscale (W3C 2023c; Rector 2019; Microsoft 2026d).
- Use few colours: at most 4 colours per chart, one per category, in a fixed order (ScotGov 2024, convention).
- For ordered data, choose a map by lightness, such as viridis, not a rainbow-like map (Crameri 2020; Matplotlib 2026).
- Keep photographs in their real colours (Bramao 2011).
- Show position and length before colour: [draw relations](https://github.com/Tewf/reader-ergonomics/blob/v1.13.0/principles/page/draw-relations.md).

## Why

Ozcelik 2009 (n = 52, abstract) found that colour-coding text and picture parts raised retention and transfer. Eye tracking showed that readers located the corresponding parts faster.

Jet-like maps can distort over 7% of the displayed data variation. An estimated 8% of men and 0.5% of women have a colour-vision deficiency (Crameri 2020, full text). Viridis gave consistently low error in 56 subjects (Liu 2018, full text).

## Limits

- A hue background that signalled lesson sections helped a structure test, d = 0.41, and not a fact test, d = -0.17 (Clariana 2009). A background colour as retrieval cue did not help recall (Meusel 2024).
- A diverging blue-orange scheme erred when a comparison crossed its centre (Liu 2018). Rainbow maps can help some inference tasks, so the ban is a default, not a law (Reda 2021, secondary).
- Pleasant colours help a little in lessons. Retention d+ = 0.387 across 33 samples (Brom 2018), but only 5 of the 33 changed colour alone, and no moderator held after correction.
- Colour harmony is preference only: no study of harmony and learning was found (Schloss 2011). Hue meaning, such as red as alarm, is untested for a live audience.
- Colour aids object recognition at d = 0.28 overall, and d = 0.18, marginal (p = .06), for objects that are not colour-diagnostic (Bramao 2011).
- A claim that colour decides up to 90% of quick purchase judgments cites a study without a working link (Ethos3 2026, 2.5 of 20, example only).
