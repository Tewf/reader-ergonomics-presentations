# Claim headline with evidence

**Claim:** each slide's headline is a sentence stating its claim, and the slide shows the evidence.

- **Grade:** supported with caveat (one controlled study of a bundle).
- **Evidence:** [review, strand 2](../../evidence/presentations/review.md); [position, Known and Not known](../../evidence/presentations/position.md).
- **Check:** `slide-headlines` (heuristic): a headline that is a topic phrase, or longer than two lines, is flagged; whether it states the claim is judgment.
- **Reader styles:** the [business decision maker](../../reader-styles/business-decision-maker.md) row on a deck read alone relies on it; [casual audience](../../reader-styles/casual-audience.md) makes slides optional, and their rules hold when there are slides.

## What it asks

- Write the headline as a sentence of at most two lines that says what the slide shows.
- Make the evidence a graphic or a table, placed with the words that explain it.
- Give each slide one claim. Leave out decorative images (Sundararajan 2020).
- Use the figure principles of the standard: [draw relations](https://github.com/Tewf/reader-ergonomics/blob/v1.10.0/principles/page/draw-relations.md) and [figure next to its text](https://github.com/Tewf/reader-ergonomics/blob/v1.10.0/principles/page/figure-next-to-its-text.md).

## Why

Garner 2013 compared two decks in a single narrated talk of 8 minutes, with 110 undergraduate engineers assigned at random. The common deck averaged 41.5 words a slide, bullets under a topic phrase. The other averaged 21.2, a graphic under a sentence headline.

- Essay scored with an analytic rubric: d = 0.81.
- Unannounced sentence completion about a week later: d = 0.89.
- Self-rated effort on 7 points: 3.01 for the sentence headlines against 3.61.

Issa 2013 found delayed transfer d = 0.83 at 1 week and 1.17 at 4 weeks for slides redesigned on Mayer's principles (abstract; random assignment not stated).

## Limits

- One experiment carries the weight: engineering students, one topic, one talk. The condition bundles six principles, so the headline, the graphic and the lower word count are not separated.
- Immediate facts and statistics showed no difference. Viewers of the sentence headlines judged their slides to have less appropriate text than the other group did.
- Alley, the main designer of the approach, is a coauthor of Garner 2013. Alley 2007 bundles the design with active learning.
- Practice disagrees on the form. Beamer 2026 advises phrases over sentences. SSP 2026 limits a title to 6 words. Both agree on short slides.
- Whether the effect holds for expert audiences, for hour-long talks and for live speakers is not known.
