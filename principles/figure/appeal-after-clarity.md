# Appeal after clarity

**Claim:** decoration stays only when it carries the message and alters no quantity; polish comes after the other figure rules hold, and never replaces them.

- **Grade:** supported with caveat (the meta-analyses are measured, for interfaces).
- **Evidence:** [review, strand 3](../../evidence/figures/review.md); [against, Minimalism against embellishment](../../evidence/figures/against.md).
- **Check:** `figure-judgment` (judgment): does any decoration distort a quantity or compete with the message?
- **Reader styles:** none changes it.

## What it asks

- Apply the other figure rules first; then choose type, colour and spacing that look good.
- Keep an image or an ornament only if it says the message.

## Why

Beauty gives a small gain in performance with interfaces: g = 0.12 over 25 studies (Thielsch 2019), g = 0.29 over 31 (Schlamann 2026). Embellished charts were described as well as plain ones and remembered better weeks later (Bateman 2010), while other embellishments slowed search or lowered accuracy (Borgo 2012; Skau 2015). Material that is interesting but irrelevant lowers learning (Cheng 2026).

## Limits

- Bateman 2010 used 20 students and charts by one designer (Few 2011; Kosara 2010).
- No study tests appeal in a node-link diagram.
